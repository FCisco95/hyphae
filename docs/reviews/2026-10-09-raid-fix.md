## Original findings: disposition

1. **Medium — cancellation/send race: resolved.** `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.ts:150` acquires the task's `FOR SHARE` lock inside the delivery transaction. The cancellation read, send at line 162 and outcome update remain in that transaction, with no unlock between recheck and send. If cancellation commits after claiming but before delivery, delivery records `skipped` / `raid_cancelled` at line 160 without invoking the sender. If delivery acquires the lock first, `transitionRaid` waits at `apps/api/src/member-journey/lifecycle.ts:73`, before acquiring the community reward lock at line 79. The delivery clock is the plain read in `apps/api/src/rewards/config.ts:189`; delivery does not take that reward row lock.

   Real-Postgres tests at `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.pg.test.ts:145` and line 159 exercise both orders through separate pools and the actual lifecycle function. The first asserts zero sends and a terminal skipped row after cancellation commits. The second gates the sender, starts cancellation while delivery holds the lock, checks cancellation has not confirmed after 500 ms, then releases delivery and observes both operations complete. The PGlite regression at `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.test.ts:196` also checks the skipped recap cannot be reclaimed.

2. **Medium — stale claim bypassing the retry deadline: resolved.** The conflict action at `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.ts:93` requires both the conflicting row's `status = 'pending'` and `next_attempt_at <= due`. It does not trust the earlier candidate read. A failed predicate returns no row and therefore no claim.

   The controlled Postgres test at `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.pg.test.ts:192` pauses a real claimant after selection and before its INSERT using a separate pool. Another claimant receives a 429 and commits a 30-second delay; the stale INSERT then returns no claim. A claim at 29,999 ms returns none, and two contenders at the deadline yield exactly one retry. The test checks two total send attempts and persisted `retryUsed: true` after success. Only the pause gate is intercepted; the conflict update and locking run on PostgreSQL.

3. **Low — unbounded 429 retries: resolved.** The first INSERT uses the default `retry_used = false`. Reclaiming a pending row atomically writes `status = 'sending'` and `retryUsed: true` at `apps/api/src/raid-alerts/recap.ts:92`, returns that flag with the claim, and commits before delivery. A second 429 becomes terminal `failed` / `rate_limit_retry_exhausted` at line 166. Later sweeps cannot claim it.

   Tests cover first 429 then success, second 429 then no claim at five later sweep times, a restarted notifier making no third request, a 60-second fallback for missing/zero/fractional `retry_after`, and a retry deadline beyond the one-hour window remaining pending without a send. Restart means stopping and recreating the notifier against the same stored state; an OS process restart was not performed. The retry state is persisted, not an in-memory counter.
